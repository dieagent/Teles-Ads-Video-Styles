# Documentary design and animation specification

This is a production interpretation of the supplied reference, grounded in [the full-size stills](../../../../examples/documentary/reference/frames/) and [the contact sheet](../../../../examples/documentary/reference/storyboard-24.jpg). Recreate its design *grammar* with original elements. The values below are starting targets for a new production, not measurements claimed for every source frame.

## Frame and composition

- Compose 1080 × 1920 vertical, about 30 fps. Put major headlines toward the upper left, generally inside a 90 px horizontal inset and away from platform UI zones. Leave a clear subject silhouette or mechanism in the middle and lower frame.
- A shot should have foreground object, midground action, and textured background. Use limited depth of field or light falloff to separate layers, then preserve enough contrast for the mechanism.
- Use close human-scale details (hands, ankle, vial), wide orientation shots (globe, room), and cutaways (body or process). Alternate visual scale when the explanation changes level, not on a timer.
- Keep a subtle vertical seam, paper grain, ink flecks, or dust where it suits the scene. Such texture unifies the rendered layers; it must not obscure evidence or text.

## Palette and light

| Purpose | Source observation | Practical production target |
|---|---|---|
| Familiar belief and setup | Amber, rusty red, parchment, warm skin light | Ochre `#D99529`, rust `#743222`, bone `#F3E8D0` |
| Classification and explanatory mechanism | Dark olive/green fields, luminous pathway | Forest `#284537`, signal yellow `#F4B72D` |
| Hazard, mistake, or lost time | Near-black, crimson, warning orange | Charcoal `#151116`, red `#A52F2D` |
| Hidden night threat | Deep navy/indigo with warm isolated lantern | Navy `#152554`, cool blue `#2E477D`, small amber accent |
| Solution and answer | Dark neutral with clearer golden/cream highlights | Cream `#F7EBD6`, gold `#D59A3C` |

Use the colors to *teach*: a chapter shift should mean a change in setting or explanation. Test white and amber type on every composite. Preserve human skin tones and evidence image fidelity when grading.

## Type and caption motion

- Use a bold condensed sans serif with uppercase English keywords over Hindi/Hinglish narration. The source uses brief cards such as `SO DOES IT WORK?`, `DRY BITE`, `IT BITES SLEEPERS` and `NOT JHAAD-PHOONK / NOT A ROPE`.
- Prefer 2–6 words per card, at most two or three stacked lines. One phrase can be white, the answer/reversal amber or red. Keep the scene readable; do not show every spoken word as a giant card.
- Type enters on the word it labels: quick vertical reveal, scale settle, or hard cut. Let it hold long enough to read, then clear on the next concept. Use a separate small citation line for provenance, not a third giant slogan.
- Retain a subtitle track for accessibility if the delivery platform needs it, with a separate type treatment and safe zone. The large cards are emphases, not a substitute for accurate captions. Do not keep branding, a title bug, or a source strip on every shot; they flatten the scene changes that make the reference work.

## Animation language

| Narrative task | Source technique | New-topic equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Establish scale | Rotating globe, India highlight, number card | Map push from world to place, dated location pin, count reveal |
| Show a false inference | Ritual image juxtaposed with a dry-bite explanation | Apparent cause and actual cause split into distinct layers |
| Explain mechanism | Glowing pathway through anatomical cutaway | Decision flow, technical cross-section, moving particles or timeline |
| Create urgency | Clock hands and colored warning lights | Deadline, sequence compression, approaching event marker |
| Change emotional chapter | Blue nighttime room, off-axis light and low snake camera | New location with purposeful palette/light reset |
| Prove a treatment or answer | Horse/antibody/vial transformation | Archival document → decision consequence, or model → verified outcome |

Animate position, depth, occlusion and cause. A moving wallpaper with static text does not meet this style. A line or box drawn on top of an image also fails when it does not move the depicted object or reveal a mechanism. Use easing for camera travel, but let hard impacts land on revelations. Revisit a visual motif at the end to close the question.

## Voice, music, sound

Direct a conversational, curious narrator. Sentence one should contain an intelligible question or surprising apparent contradiction. Vary intensity with the argument: setup inviting, mechanism precise, danger lower and tighter, answer clear. Write in spoken Hindi/Hinglish, not literal translation of an English essay. Let the narrator pronounce names and dates naturally.

Keep music beneath speech and duck it further under names, dates, figures and caveats. Use a limited cue set: low hit for a claim, ticking/pulse for time, subtle whoosh for a spatial change, softer release when the answer arrives. Leave a small breath or reaction beat only where an image or sound does work. Verify the final mix by watching the export with sound; a score file alone cannot prove clarity.

## Source image inserts

If a document screenshot is used, preserve the true page in the archive and create a *designed insert* only from that page: crop to title/publisher/key line, surround with the same paper/film treatment, and animate a push/highlight to the exact supported words. Hold enough time to read. Add a legible publisher/date citation and the direct URL in the source ledger. The screenshot is evidence, not generic background decoration. See [the Japan archive capture](../../../../examples/documentary/japan-atomic-bombings/archive-page.png).

## Quality gate

At draft review, compare a three-second sample sheet against the source. Check every 3–5 seconds: what did the voice just say, what on screen demonstrates it, and what changed since the last beat? If the answer is only “the camera zoomed,” replace the shot. A two-minute cut should have many distinct compositions and animated changes within them, rather than looping a few plates. Check one phone-size frame per scene, spellings, dates, source credits, subtitle placement, voice/music balance and the final question.
