## Specific edit behavior

The first section progresses from a familiar claim to a question before giving the answer. The middle uses **visual causality**: geography becomes risk; a bite becomes a dry-bite explanation; venom becomes a moving biological path; a clock represents lost treatment time. The night section is a deliberate location/palette reset, then the antivenom section returns to brighter explanation. Captions are short explanatory headlines, not continuous full subtitles. A few words can occupy a large fraction of the upper half of frame, but the illustrated action remains visible underneath or beside them.

The clip blends stylized 3D-ish renders, cutout/diagram layers, shallow light and textured surfaces. It does not feel like stock footage under captions. The camera pushes, tracks, changes depth and perspective, while internal objects move: globe rotation, snake approach, fluid/nerve path, clock hand, vial/antibody reveal. Hard cuts and quick graphic swaps occur at new ideas; related shots continue a spatial or visual action. Avoid arbitrary zooms on each word.

## Sound and speech

Hindi/Hinglish narration carries one connected argument. It appears almost continuous across the 127 seconds; the edit is timed to spoken claims rather than waiting through large silent title-card gaps. The soundtrack supports the narration with restrained music and punctuating cues; exact voice levels and cue timings need a fresh listening pass during production. Never infer a perfectly accurate transcript or medical guidance from automated transcription. Lock the final cut to a recorded voice take, then listen at phone volume to catch masking or late on-screen claims.

## Transfer to a different subject

For history, replace the anatomy pathway with an animated chronology or decision diagram. For science, use a cross-section or process. Keep the **relationship** between narrative claim and changing image: one question, visible mechanism, evidence, consequence, answer. Do not force snake imagery, medical treatment, an exact running time or the source's English phrases into an unrelated film.
